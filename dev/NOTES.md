# Notes

What was learned about the game's lighting while this was DAOLib's `examples/graphics` (issue 1 here, which was DAOLib's 65, until 2026-10-11). Addresses are GOG's 1.05; the IDs are the address library's.

## What lights a figure

- **Point lights.** A figure's shader is handed `g_vLightParams`, thirteen registers at c0: point lights as a place and reach and a colour, at [0]/[1], [5]/[6] and [7]/[8] (three "flagged" lights) and [9]/[11] (one "unflagged"); the sun's direction and colour at [2]/[3]. A colour's fourth component is passed to the shader and no shader of the game's reads it as a number, so the plugin carries a light's strength there.
- **Fills.** An area keeps three far-off lights (a reach of 1000 m in the Circle) for its figures alone, the same everywhere in it. They are what makes figures flat. `FillsLess` turns them down where the area has no sun; under a sun they stand for the sky.
- **Lamps.** A level's own lamps light its walls and never its figures in vanilla. `Lamps` offers a figure those of 2 to 100 m reach that are near it.
- **Ambient light.** A figure's ambient colour comes from a light probe's irradiance (`g_mLightParams`, three 4 by 4 matrices, Ramamoorthi and Hanrahan's over pi), made a colour per vertex. The engine chooses the one nearest probe of the figure's room, with no blending: it switches in a frame.
- **Portraits and the inventory's figure** are each a scene of their own with their own light manager and three lights of some 200 m. Only the current area's scene (`CCArea::Scene`, the owner at +8 of its light manager) has fills.

## The engine's light manager

`Renderer::LightManagerDefault` (vftable 0x00AE7BF0) has six lists. `AddLights` (2701) sorts a point light by kind into the level's pair (+0x24 flagged, +0xC unflagged) or the figures' pair (+0x6C, +0x84); light probes go to +0x3C and ambient lights to +0x54. `ChooseForMoving` (2694) chooses a figure's lights through `LightList::ChooseFor` (2691), each light scored by its virtual at +0x84: a point light's score (7628) is its strength times what is left of its reach, a probe's (7629) one over its distance, both nothing for a light of another room.

The chosen lights go into the figure's light state (groups at +0, +0x10, +0x20 for probes, +0x30). `Renderer::LightState::Fill` (2848) makes of them what the shaders are handed: the point lights at +0x40, the probe's cube map at +0x114, its three matrices at +0x130, +0x170, +0x1B0, copied from the probe's blueprint only when the probe is not the one kept at +0x110. The plugin writes its own mix over the matrices after that function.

## A level's files

- A level is a folder of `packages\core\env`. `<level>.rim` has each room's definition (`<level>_<room>.rml`); each room's own archives its layout (`<level>_<room>_ll.mmh`: its lamps, its ambient light, its light probe) and its lightmap atlases (DXT5, up to 1024 a side; the alpha is where the sun reaches).
- Models' trees are in `modelhierarchies.erf` and their meshes in `modelmeshdata.erf`. A mesh node's part id is what a room's lightmap list goes by.
- A level's shader lays the lightmap on at its face value: surface = texture times (lightmap + sun times the lightmap's alpha + ambient).
- An area's level is named in its `.are` (in `designerareas.erf`), not by the area's name. The mage origin's Circle is `lak303d`, the Arl of Denerim's dungeon `den011d`, the Dead Trenches `orz900d`, Orzammar's Commons `orz100d`.
- **The lamps of a layout are not what the lightmaps were baked from.** By the toolset's lightmapper's own formula (EclipseRay's `pointlight.cc`: colour times one less the distance over the radius, by the cosine) they give 6% of how a floor's lightmap varies. The lightmaps were baked from lights of the level editor's that did not ship.
- Of 158 levels, 662 rooms have a light probe of the level's own, and a five-metre grid over their floors is about 23,000 probes. Most rooms of a level made of terrain (Orzammar's Commons) have none.
- **A level is of rooms or of terrain** (106 and 52 of the 158). A terrain level is everything out of doors and Orzammar's halls: its rooms are tiles (`<level>_<x>_<y>_0.rml`, the last number the detail), its ground patches of two tiles by two, each a model one of its tiles places (`<level>_<n>_0.msh`, in that tile's own `.rim`; one set of texture coordinates, the lightmap's; its corners in the other order from a room's floor), and it has one probe for all of it, seldom two or three, in `<level>_ll.mmh`, which the level's own `<level>.rml` places. The engine gives a figure anywhere in the level that probe.
- **A probe's cube map is its photograph**, and its `.mtx` the nine coefficients of that picture: Direct3D's six faces in the level's own axes (z up), each with its smaller pictures after it, the values as they are stored. Redcliffe's reckoned so are the file's to 0.003.
- A lightmap out of doors is one dim colour over a whole village (41, 52, 41 of 255 in Redcliffe); the sun's shadows are in its alpha.

## The probes the plugin bakes

A lightmap is the light a surface receives, the lamps' own and the bounce both. So each side of a figure is lit as the level's surface behind it is: rays go out from a place a metre above the floor, the lightmap is read where each lands, and its colour is counted as light arriving from the ray's opposite side. A level's own probes hold the bounce alone (a camera's picture of the surfaces about, about 0.6 of the lightmaps seen), which is why vanilla needs the fills and why the look lifts a level's own probe (`Ambient`) and not a baked one.

A terrain level's probes are made another way, since its lightmaps say so little: as its own probe was made. From the level's own probe's photograph the baker takes the sky (wherever a ray from the probe's place lands on nothing) and what a lit surface of the level sends on (so much of its baked light, so much of the sun where the lightmap's alpha says it reaches, so much with neither, fitted to what the photograph shows of the surfaces about); a probe anywhere else is the photograph taken again by reckoning. At the level's own probe that gives the level's own back: 0.998 like it and 1.02 as bright in Redcliffe, 0.999 and 0.98 in Denerim, 0.98 and 0.92 in Orzammar's Commons. Such a probe holds what a level's own does, the bounce and the sky, and is lifted like one where there is no sun; a room's holds all the light and is not. Places are every floor with room to stand, eight metres apart: Redcliffe 3,936, the Commons 2,818.

The baker reads a level's archives on a thread in Windows' background mode, bakes every room that has a probe of the level's own (the engine's choice of that probe is how a figure's room is known, by the probe's place and its light, which tells a village by day from the same by night), and hands the level over whole. A table is kept in a file and read when the player next comes into the level; the nearest few probes are found through a grid, a metre of height counting for three. The mage origin's Circle is 269 probes in 2.3 s from the command line; the same in play, 4 to 5 s; the Dead Trenches' 4,573 in 5.5 s. A figure is given the nearest four, each by the square of how far one over its distance is above the fifth's.

## The picture

- The scene is drawn into a 16-bit target of the plugin's own and brought back to 8 bits through a shoulder: its own to a knee, then a curve that is white at a range the plugin sets by the area's sun (one and a half whites with none, 5.1 at a sun of 3), hue kept to 0.4 of the range and burnt out by the whole of it. The game's colour grade, which runs after, is still 0 to 1, and there is no HDR output: that wants a presentation that is not Direct3D 9's.
- **What makes a day out of doors flat is the light's ratios, and the engine has what it takes to change them** (2026-10-11, after the user's "we need some kind of lighting overhaul to really make the scenes look realistic and punchy"). An area's sun is hardly brighter than its shade: at three times its own (`Sun`), with the sun on figures at twice (`SunOnFigures`) and the picture's range to hold it, ground lies in sun and shade is shade. The area's fog hides most of the rest. Redcliffe's fog is a dark grey-green capped at 0.85 and its sky is drawn at one part in a hundred (`atmosphere alpha` 0.01), under which the engine has a whole reckoned sky: a sun's colour and strength, haze and air (Mie, Rayleigh), clouds with a colour and a density (`dao/core/Sky.h`, 27 values, each set live). With the fog given the sun's colour (`Haze`), its cap and thickness taken down (`Clear`), the clouds lit (`Clouds`) and the sky shown (`Sky`), the village stands under an evening sky with its castle in the sun. A look can set any of the 27 outright to try it: `SkyFogCap = 0.4`.
- **A moon is a sun to the engine, and what the sun falls on decides how much of it a picture holds.** Three times the camp's moon was a day; a moon's light has half as much red as blue (0.25, 0.37, 0.48) and a sun's three quarters or more, the coldest of them over snow, so under a moon nothing is made stronger. And snow in a sun four times the area's own is burnt out where a forest's floor is well lit: the plugin measures how much of the picture's middle is at white, every eighth frame on a copy 128 across, and holds the look's sun back where it is more than four parts in a hundred, slowly letting it back. Bringing the exposure down instead left the shade beside the snow black.
- **Nothing the game draws is brighter than white unless it is made so**, so a glow of what is over the picture's range had nothing to spread: a campfire and a lamp are pictures of light, no brighter than a pale wall. What burns is made brighter where it is drawn (`Fire`: the effects' shaders and lava's, by how bright and how coloured a pixel is, since the same shaders draw smoke and the pale haze over Orzammar's lava, which made brighter was a white wall), and the glow (`Bloom`) is six passes of ours in the grade's chain: what is over 0.55 of the range at a quarter of the size, spread, spread again into a sixteenth, and the two summed for the grade to add before its shoulder. A target of the chain's may be `BACKBUFFER_WIDTH/16`.
- **The air lit by the sun** (`SunHaze`): light added by how much air a pixel is seen through (the occlusion's depth in metres; the sky is all of it) and by how nearly the pixel looks at the sun. Where the sun is in the picture is found with no reading of the camera: points far off along ways in the world are asked their place on the screen (`dao::world::ToScreen`), two of them give the picture's focal length and the way to the sun its place. Towards the sun Redcliffe's sky is a bright warm glare with its trees dark against it; away from it nothing changes.
- **Shafts of light** (`Shafts`): a pass among the glow's, a quarter of the picture's size, in which each pixel looks along the picture towards the sun's place in it and counts how much of the way is open sky or far haze (from the depth in metres, 28 steps, each pixel starting a part of a step along); the glow's last pass makes that light, the sun's colour by how nearly the pixel looks at the sun. Past Redcliffe's gallows and its cliff the light comes down in beams. The air's light and the shafts are held back with the sun by the meter, or a sky burnt out towards the sun would hold the ground's sun down and stay burnt.
- **Every lit surface but the ground is cut at white in its own shader**, each channel on its own, just before the fog (`mul_sat <colour>.xyz, <colour>, <tint>` in the shaders of buildings, furnishings and figures; the terrain's have none): strong warm light on a wall was pale yellow before the picture's roll-off saw it. With the look's Highlights on the cut is taken out (`DaoCut`).
- **A level's shader is handed real lights and one flat colour.** Its block is a figure's: point lights at [0]/[1], [5]/[6], [7]/[8], [9]/[11], the sun at [2]/[3], and at [4] the area's ambient colour, added to the lights' sum. A level's layouts hold its lights as `nplt` nodes (Orzammar's Commons has 60: fires of 10 to 40 m, and one named `lava` of 100 m whose colour is black). With the baked light and the ambient colour both taken away (`BakedLess`, `AmbientLess`) and those lamps at four times (`LampsOnLevel`), the Commons is a dark hall with the lava's opening glowing behind it and the Circle a hall lit by its candles, which is the first thing of the day that looks more interesting and not only different (the user, 2026-10-11: "I don't think we're going to get there by adjusting, contrast or whatever. I think we want more interesting lighting"; "What if we got rid of baked in ambient light? That would force us to relight properly"; "Can we add our own lights? ... there should be light from the windows"). What is wrong with it as it stands: a mesh takes four lights at the most, so floors are lit in patches with hard edges; no lamp casts a shadow; much is black; and figures keep a key light of their own indoors (`on creatures (0.71, 0.50, 0.29)` in an area whose sun is black), bright in a dark room.
- **Raising the baked light's contrast is not it** (`LightContrast`, parked at 0): a bake that is one even colour, Orzammar's, only gets brighter.
- One exposure does not serve a room and a day: the look's `Exposure` counts only where the area has a sun, and a room is left as its makers lit it. With a shoulder long enough for the boosted sun everywhere, the Circle was a third darker.
- Shaders are not loaded again when a save is: a shader change needs the game started again.
- The lights' registers are set on a render thread that replays recorded commands, so a stack walked there does not reach the game's code that chose them.

- **The ambient occlusion shaded all of every surface's light wherever the game anti-aliases (found 2026-10-12; issue 8, which was closed and was not done; issue 16 for the way it ought to be done).** The user reported one plumed helm as see-through six times in a day. It was four things, found in this order, the last the largest:
  - The helm drawn blended whole for its feathers' tips, its parts over one another in the order of its triangles: a material the table knows for a solid is drawn cut out (`cut_out`, `is_cut_out`).
  - Its metalness map ending where the paint's shade began: plate in shade is plate (`metal_map`'s `plate`).
  - The alpha shaders, which draw cut-outs too, saying no share at all.
  - **And no share arriving from anything.** It was written to the distance target's alpha (the second target). The game binds that target beside the picture only when it does NOT anti-alias. With `AntialiasingLevel` over 0 (8 in the user's copy and the agent's) the picture's target has samples, a second target cannot stand beside it and be read afterwards, and the game draws the distance in a pass of its own before the scene: a traced frame at 8 samples is 118 draws into the scene's target, a copy to the distance texture, a clear, the scene's 348 draws into one target, and never a second target; with anti-aliasing off it is 64 draws for the depth alone and then the scene into two targets. Proved by a share forced to a quarter in every shader: read back as 1 everywhere at 8 samples, as a quarter with anti-aliasing off. `build_shaders.py <out> <size> show-share` is the view that shows it: the openness in red, the share in green, their product in blue.
  - Where the share rides now: the picture's own alpha (`with_ambient_share`), which is there in both modes; the occlusion pass reads it from the chain's copy of the picture. A forced quarter reads back as a quarter at 8 samples. Vanilla's solid shaders write their opacity there, 1; of the game's 178 render states the three that blend by a target's alpha are the distortion's (`DistortionMask.sta`, `WeaponTrail.sta`, a test), which draws into a target of its own. A cut-out's alpha is what its state tests, so its passes are pointed at a copy of the shader that cuts itself out (`clip`) and a copy of the state with the test off (`find_solid`, `with_solid_shaders`: eight shaders, `DaoCharacterPT.sta`). What is drawn blended (hair, wings, particles) says nothing and what is behind it keeps a blend of the two alphas; the fog a shader lays on is shaded with the rest.
