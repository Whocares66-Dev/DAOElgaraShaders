; What a material is by its names: the tables the table builder and the plugin both class a
; material by (issue 19). Edited by hand. Lines are tab-separated:
;
;   table <worn|creatures|weapons|things> <class> <fallback or -> <words>
;   tileset <class> <codes>
;   sound <number> <class>
;   wielded <pattern>
;   worn_texture <pattern>
;   clear <class> <what it mirrors face on>
;   clear_power <the power a clear solid shines by>
;
; A table is read from its top and the first row whose words match says. The text matched is
; the material's name, its diffuse texture's stem and its project, in lower case. Each word is
; a regular expression (PCRE2, which reads Python's as Python does); the words are one
; pattern joined by |. A class of `paint` says the textures say how much of the thing is
; metal, and the fallback is what the rest of it is.

; The clear solids, and how much light goes more slowly in each than in air (its index of
; refraction: window glass 1.5, quartz 1.55, corundum, a ruby or a sapphire, 1.77, ice 1.31;
; any handbook of optics). What such a thing mirrors face on is ((n - 1) / (n + 1)) squared,
; and it is smooth. The game's textures cannot say either: a crystal's and an ice wall's
; masks have no gloss at all (0.00 to 0.05, a power of 1 to 13: read 2026-10-12), being
; painted to glow and not to shine, and by them the model drew a crystal as matte as chalk.
; So for these the table says what they are and the shader takes the substance's mirror
; and the top of the game's own scale of gloss (CLEAR_POWER) in place of the mask's.
clear	glass	0.0400
clear	crystal	0.0465
clear	gem	0.0773
clear	ice	0.0180
clear_power	256.00
